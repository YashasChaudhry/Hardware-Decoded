PCIe 4.0 vs 5.0 SSDs: Is Gen 5 Worth It in 2026?

PCIe 5.0 SSDs are nearly twice as fast on paper — but do you actually notice? We compare Gen 4 and Gen 5 NVMe drives for gaming, work, heat, and value.

PCIe 5.0 SSDs advertise sequential read speeds of 12,000 to 14,500 MB/s — nearly double what the best PCIe 4.0 drives manage. It sounds like a huge upgrade, and the price tag usually reflects that. But speed on a spec sheet isn't the same as speed you can feel. In this guide we'll explain what PCIe generations actually mean, where Gen 5 genuinely helps, where it doesn't, and which one you should buy.

What Does the PCIe Generation Actually Mean?

Every Generation Doubles the Bandwidth

PCIe is the connection between your motherboard and components like your graphics card and your NVMe SSD. Each new generation doubles the bandwidth per lane. A standard M.2 NVMe SSD uses four lanes, which means PCIe 4.0 tops out at roughly 8 GB/s in theory and PCIe 5.0 at roughly 16 GB/s.

In real products, top PCIe 4.0 drives such as the Samsung 990 Pro and WD Black SN850X reach around 7,300–7,450 MB/s on reads, while flagship PCIe 5.0 drives reach roughly 12,000–14,500 MB/s. For comparison, a SATA SSD tops out near 550 MB/s and an older PCIe 3.0 NVMe drive around 3,500 MB/s.

Quick stat: A top PCIe 5.0 SSD can be more than 20 times faster than a SATA drive in raw sequential reads — but you'll rarely see that in daily use.

It's Backwards Compatible

You can put a PCIe 5.0 SSD in a PCIe 4.0 slot and it will work, but only at PCIe 4.0 speeds. The reverse works too: a PCIe 4.0 drive in a Gen 5 slot runs at Gen 4 speeds. So you won't break anything by mixing generations — you'll just waste money if the drive can't use its full speed.

Quick tip: Newer platforms like AMD AM5 and recent Intel boards offer Gen 5 M.2 slots, but not every board in those families has one. Check your motherboard manual before buying.

Does Gen 5 Actually Feel Faster?

Gaming: Almost No Difference

This is the big one. In real game testing, load times between a good PCIe 4.0 SSD and a PCIe 5.0 SSD are nearly identical — usually a second or two at most, and often much less. Modern games are limited by how the game engine processes data and decompresses assets, not by raw SSD bandwidth.

Even DirectStorage, which was supposed to make fast SSDs matter more, hasn't turned Gen 5 into a gaming upgrade yet. Once you're in a game, your frame rate is decided by your GPU and CPU, not your SSD.

Recommendation: If you mainly game, buy a fast PCIe 4.0 SSD and put the savings into your GPU.

Everyday Use: Boot Times, Apps, Browsing

Windows boot time, app launches, and web browsing show no perceptible difference between Gen 4 and Gen 5. Everyday tasks rely heavily on small random reads, and good Gen 4 drives already handle those extremely well.

This is why upgrading from a SATA SSD to a decent NVMe drive feels like a big step, but going from Gen 4 to Gen 5 usually feels like nothing.

Creative and Professional Work: Where Gen 5 Can Help

Gen 5 does make a real difference if you regularly move very large files, such as 100GB+ video projects between drives. Workloads like loading large AI models and processing big datasets can also benefit. In those cases the extra bandwidth saves actual time.

Even then, your other storage or network on the other end of the transfer can become the bottleneck, so check that the rest of your workflow can keep up.

Recommendation: Gen 5 makes sense for video editors, data scientists, and anyone working with huge files all day.

The Downsides of Gen 5

1. Heat

Gen 5 SSDs run hot. Most models need a proper heatsink, and some need active cooling, or they'll throttle — slowing themselves down — during long transfers. Many motherboards include M.2 heatsinks, but a heatsink alone doesn't help if your case airflow is poor or a big GPU is sitting right above the slot.

A Gen 4 SSD with a basic heatsink generally stays cool and consistent.

Quick tip: A good heatsink or thermal pad can be the difference between full speed and frustrating slowdowns on a Gen 5 drive.

2. Price

Gen 5 drives cost noticeably more than Gen 4 drives of the same capacity, and flash storage prices have also been under pressure from the same AI-driven demand that's pushing RAM prices up. Check live prices rather than trusting any number in an article, including this one.

A top Gen 4 drive typically delivers nearly all of the real-world performance at a much lower price.

Quick stat: In most tests, the best Gen 4 drives deliver around 98% of the real-world experience of Gen 5 at roughly half the price.

3. Platform Requirements

To get Gen 5 speeds you need a CPU and motherboard that support them, and the specific M.2 slot you use has to be a Gen 5 slot. If you're on an older platform, you'd be paying for speed you can't use.

Which SSD Should You Buy?

Gamers and Everyday Users

Buy a high-quality PCIe 4.0 NVMe SSD. You'll get excellent performance, lower heat, and a much better price. A 2TB Gen 4 drive is a better purchase than a 1TB Gen 5 drive for almost everyone, because running out of space hurts more than a slightly slower drive.

Recommendation: Prioritise capacity and quality over generation.

Creators and Professionals

If you work with huge files daily and your motherboard has a Gen 5 slot, Gen 5 can be worth it — especially if the price gap is small. Make sure you have proper cooling for the drive.

Budget Builders

A reliable mid-range Gen 4 drive or even a good Gen 3 drive is plenty for most budget builds. Don't spend extra on speed you won't feel.

Buy PCIe 4.0 if:

  • ✅ You mostly game, study, or browse
  • ✅ You want the best value per dollar
  • ✅ You'd rather spend the savings on a bigger drive or a better GPU
  • ✅ You don't want to worry about heat and throttling

Buy PCIe 5.0 if:

  • ✅ You regularly move 100GB+ files
  • ✅ Your motherboard has a Gen 5 M.2 slot
  • ✅ You have a heatsink and decent case airflow for the drive
  • ✅ The price gap over Gen 4 is small

Common mistakes to avoid:

  • 🔍 Choosing a drive based only on its peak sequential speed
  • 🔍 Installing a Gen 5 drive without a heatsink
  • 🔍 Buying a smaller Gen 5 drive instead of a larger Gen 4 drive
  • 🔍 Putting a Gen 5 drive in a slot that only runs at Gen 4 speeds

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a PCIe 5.0 SSD worth it for gaming?

No. Game load times between a good PCIe 4.0 SSD and a PCIe 5.0 SSD are nearly identical, so Gen 5 gives no meaningful gaming advantage today.

Will a PCIe 5.0 SSD work in a PCIe 4.0 slot?

Yes, it will work, but it will run at PCIe 4.0 speeds.

Do PCIe 5.0 SSDs need a heatsink?

Almost always. Gen 5 drives run hot and can throttle without proper cooling, so a good heatsink is strongly recommended.

Should I buy a bigger Gen 4 SSD or a smaller Gen 5 SSD?

For almost everyone, the bigger Gen 4 drive is the better buy.

Bottom line: PCIe 5.0 SSDs are impressively fast on paper, but for gaming and everyday use you won't feel the difference over a good PCIe 4.0 drive — and you'll pay more and deal with extra heat. Gen 5 is a smart pick for creators and professionals moving huge files on a compatible board. For everyone else, buy a quality Gen 4 SSD in the biggest capacity you can afford.
